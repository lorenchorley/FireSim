## 0. Decisions (defaults, each labelled; the owner may change any)

D1. **A user change gets its own cards, not the forecast's.** A "wind change coming" card and a Dead man zone card (the plan's "Danger zone" is the in-app **Dead man zone**, layer id `dmz`; no rename) are raised for a change of the user's, from the engine's own rules, with their own keys (`wind-change:user:<id>`, `dead-man-zone:user:<id>`) so a shown forecast card never swallows them. Same thresholds as the forecast's (a turn of 45 degrees or more, a wind of 15 km/h or more after it).
D2. **Severity `watch`, not `danger`, for a change you made yourself.** The Danger gate (vibrate, pause on Danger) is for what comes at you; pausing the run right after you pressed Apply would be a pop-up of our own making.
D3. **The Dead man zone is meaningful for a user change**: same sweep (Mk5 under the new wind from the current perimeter, 5 minutes, 60 m grid), same horizon (a change at most 60 minutes away, gone when the turn starts, refreshed every 5 minutes). Its words say what it does and does not claim (1.4).
D4. **The Danger-gate gap is closed by a record field, `WindDirectionEdit.madeAt?`** (the view time the user pressed Apply), so that what explain knows is a pure function of the record list and the step time. Cards raised before `madeAt` **stay as history** (not retired).
D5. **"Back to the forecast" = remove every Wind direction change as if never made** (the same engine path as the bin button, once, from the earliest start); not a "release" swing, not the belt-kit readings. A secondary button under the list, shown while the list is not empty.
D6. **`WIND_TURN_STEP_DEG` stays 15.** Nothing measured says otherwise; the Danger-gate work adds no stamps and no background solves.
D7. **F7 (`flowGlyphs` hash): results are not changed.** It collides exactly (one cell in three has a twin) but the twin is the point reflection through the world origin, which no one can see; a fix would move every glyph for nothing visible. Comment and a pinning test only (2.7). Owner question 9 asks.
D8. **F6: own the two-finger turn with public API only** (`update()`, `enableRotate`, `touches`), in a new `src/render/twoFingerTurn.ts`; `touches.TWO` stays `DOLLY_ROTATE`, so the existing structure test and the 54 sign tests pass unchanged.
D9. **F8: the whole `reasons` block (3-D button first, then the words) moves directly under the chips in the Air slices tool**; the Layers panel's callout is already directly under its chips, so it only gets a pin.
D10. **F3's cause is the pinned footer, not the compass button** (2.3); the fix is the scroll-padding of the tool body, 18 more px of reserve, no new CSS bytes.

## 1. Item D: Wind direction follow-ups

### 1.1 What the pipeline does today

- **Engine** (`src/explain/engine.ts`). `InsightEngine.update(s)` runs every 60 s of simulated time (`E.cadenceS`). It takes the forecast analysis (`analyseSeries`, cached per series object: `detectWindChanges`, `afternoonPeaks`) and, when `s.windOverrideFromMs` is set, keeps only `changes` with `time < from` (line 169 to 172). `ctx.prepare(...)` hands that to the rules. Cards go through the per-key machine idle, armed, shown, cooldown (hysteresis 0.8, at most 3 new per cycle, kind-level de-dup radius per rule).
- **Rules** (`src/explain/rules/weather.ts`). `nextChange(ctx, horizonS)` = the first change with time in [now - 1200 s, now + horizon]. `detectWindChange`: horizon 3 h, key `wind-change:domain`, severity `danger`, persistence 1, the exposed flank (front cells whose outward normal is within 45 degrees of the new wind-to) gives `flankM` and the card's place. `detectDmz`: horizon 1 h, calls `ctx.services.dmz` (the `DmzComputer`, `safety.ts`) when `due` (first time, every 300 s, or the change moved), clears the layer when the change is past (`c.time < nowMs`), key `dead-man-zone:domain`, `danger`.
- **Forecast** (`forecast.ts`). `detectWindChanges`: circular-mean windows an hour either side of each stamp, a run of hours with a shift of 45 degrees or more, post speed 15 km/h or more, merged under 2 h; `buildForecastInsights` makes the early cards (key `wind-change:domain`, `inS` the lead).
- **Numbers** (`params.ts`): `windChange {minShiftDeg 45, minPostKmh 15, horizonS 10800, mergeS 7200, flankAngleDeg 45, holdAfterS 1200}`, `dmz {horizonS 3600, minutes 5, gridM 60, refreshS 300, minCells 1}`.
- **The Dead man zone** (`safety.ts DmzComputer.compute`): a Dijkstra sweep on a 60 m grid from the burning perimeter, speeds from the three-speed ellipse (Mk5 ROS from T and RH at `change.time` and FFDI at `change.postSpeed`, slope factors, length to breadth), the new wind-to being `toDir + 180`; the cells reached within 5 minutes go to `layers().dmz` (0 to 1, 1 at the perimeter) and the card says the area. It needs exactly three inputs: `time`, `toDir`, `postSpeed`. The card's words: "If the wind changed now, fire could reach anywhere in the shaded zone within 5 minutes ... The model leaves out the fire's own winds here, so the real zone is at least this big."
- **What the view gives it.** `SimStateView.windOverrideFromMs` (`core/simTypes.ts:329`) is `startMs + wxFromS * 1000`, and `wxFromS` is set in `Simulation.refreshWx()` (`simulation.ts:981-989`) from `activeEdits(records, applied)`: **only records already applied** (a record applies at the first step whose time is at or after its own `time`). `series` stays the forecast.
- **UI.** `Session.forecastCards` (`session.ts:800`) drops the forecast's own early cards from the first change on, using the session's edit list, so a change scheduled for later counts at once. `visibleInsights` and `mapInsights` list the rest. The Danger gate is `DangerGate` (`insightGroups.ts`, used in `simScreen.ts:889-897`): for a revealed card of severity `danger` it says `notify` (haptics) and `pause` (the opt-in "pause on danger", first card of a kind only). The demo engine (`mockSim.ts detectInsights`, 620-648) raises a `dead-man-zone`-kind card 30 minutes before each kept forecast swing and filters with `windDirectionEdits(this.edits).filter(e => e.time <= t)`, the same applied-only rule.
- **The gap, precisely.** At 10:00 the user schedules a turn for 10:40 ("In 40 min"). The forecast has a swing at 11:20. Until 10:40 `windOverrideFromMs` is absent in the engine, so the swing at 11:20, which the turn replaces, raises a `wind-change` Danger card and the `dmz` layer (from 3 h and 1 h ahead). At 10:40 the record applies, the candidate vanishes, the card goes to cooldown and the layer clears. The cards already raised stay in `state.insights`. The Weather tab, chart, timeline and the forecast cards of the Session already know (they read the session's list).

### 1.2 Design (1): a card, a zone and "Back to the forecast" for a change you made

**Data the engine needs.** A list of the changes it may count, each as the forecast's `WindChange` shape. The simulation builds it (it already imports `scenario/windDirection`; explain must not, `explain/deps.ts` is its only door):

```ts
// core/simTypes.ts, additive; SimStateView gets: windUserChanges?: readonly SimWindChange[]
export interface SimWindChange { id: string; time: number /* unix ms the turn starts */; fromDir: number; toDir: number; preSpeed: number; postSpeed: number /* m/s */ }
```
`fromDir` is the direction in force at the start (`planSwings`: the earlier swings' value, else the forecast's 10 m direction), `toDir` the edit's `dir`, `postSpeed` the forecast speed 15 minutes after the start (`weatherAt(scenario.weather, t0 + WIND_TURN_S * 1000).windSpeed10`; a turn leaves the speed alone), `preSpeed` at the start. In `InsightEngine.update`: `changes = [...kept forecast changes, ...user changes that pass the thresholds]` sorted by time (`WindChange` gets `user?: string`, the id). `nextChange` then picks the next of either sort; nothing else in the machine changes. A turn of under 45 degrees, or a wind of under 15 km/h after it, raises nothing (a "Hold wind from NW" never does).

**Keys and severity** (`rules/weather.ts`): `detectWindChange` uses `wind-change:user:<id>` and severity `watch` when `c.user`, else as now; `detectDmz` `dead-man-zone:user:<id>`, `watch`. Both add `user: 1` to `values`. A separate key means a shown forecast card does not hold the machine for the user's (one card per key); `dedupRadiusM` is 0 for both rules; the Session's duplicate window keys on `key` so it keeps them apart.

**What the card says** (`explain/text.ts`, the `windChange` and `deadManZone` renderers, `values.user` picks the variant; plain voice, "about", no em dashes). Title "Your wind change: this flank becomes the head". Body with the flank: "You set the wind to swing from the north-west to the south-west, starting at about 2:40 pm. That is in about 10 min. It takes 15 minutes to turn. When it has, this 1.2 km flank becomes the front and runs at full speed almost at once. A wide front moves fast and is hard to stop." Without a flank: "You set the wind to swing from the north-west to the south-west, starting at about 2:40 pm (about 25 km/h). When it has turned, the long side of the fire facing the new wind becomes the front and runs at full speed almost at once." Factors: New wind, Wind shift, "Starts at about 2:40 pm" (label `Change time` kept), Exposed flank. `safety`, `source`, `confidence`, `showLayers ['wind','dmz']` as the forecast card's. The "That is in ..." sentence only while the start is ahead.
**Dead man zone card, user variant:** "If the wind had already turned, fire could reach anywhere in the shaded zone within 5 minutes. The change you set, to the south-west, starts in about 10 min and takes 15 minutes to turn. The zone covers about 40 ha. The model leaves out the fire's own winds, which could make it bigger, and the slow turn means the first minutes reach less than this." (The lane may tighten; it must not say "at least" without the second half.)

**Is the Dead man zone meaningful for a user change? Yes, with two honest limits.** The physics is the same (the zone is "what the new wind alone would reach in 5 minutes from the perimeter now"), the three inputs exist, and for training it is the point: the instructor sees the exposure before the turn happens. Limits: (a) it assumes the full new wind at once, while a turn eases over 15 minutes (smoothstep: 26 % of the arc after 5 minutes, 74 % after 10), so for the first minutes it overstates; the wording says so. (b) It is shown only while the start is ahead and at most an hour away, and is cleared at the start; a "Now" change (a start within 59 s) has none. A preview from the dial before pressing Apply would be a different feature (an engine query for a hypothetical change): not built, owner question 5.

**Timeline mark** (`scrubber.ts`): one `tl-mark-change` at each user change's start, always shown (the user knows it); `marksKey` takes the changes' ids and times. **Help text** (docs lane): the sentence "A change of yours raises no wind change card and no Danger zone" (`content.ts`, pinned by `content.test.ts:804`) is replaced.

### 1.3 Design (2): closing the Danger-gate gap, and the cards raised before it

**The rule it must keep:** explain output is a function of the record list and the step time (replays reproduce the first run; no wall clock, no "when it was added"). Knowing a pending record from the start of the run would break it (a run where it was added at 10:00 and a run that had it from 09:00 would differ before 10:00), and so would a hook outside the records.

**Design:** `WindDirectionEdit.madeAt?: number` (s, the view time when the user pressed Apply, at most `time`; absent means `time`, so scenario edits, tests and the harness behave as today). `Session.setWindDirection` sets it from the view time it already reads. `madeAt` is part of the record, so a replay from any checkpoint earlier than it reproduces the first run exactly; it is **not** in `refreshWx`'s key and does not reach `wx`, the atmosphere, the fire or any snapshot hash (the series still swaps at the start, so "bitwise before T" holds).

In `Simulation.view(time)`: `known = knownWindChanges(time)` = the `windDirection` edits of `this.records` (insertion order, a later record with the same id replaces an earlier) that are applied, or have `min(madeAt ?? time, time) <= time` (a pending record made by now). Then `windOverrideFromMs` = the earliest known start (this closes the gap: the forecast swings after it are left out from the first cycle at or after `madeAt`), and `windUserChanges` = `planSwings(scenario.weather, startMs, known)` mapped as in 1.2. Memoised on `[id, dir, time]` of `known` (it is evaluated about once a minute of simulated time, plus `stateView()`). `refreshWx`, `weatherNow`, `rebindWind`, `restore` are not touched.

**Removal.** `undoRecords` for a pending or past change keeps its meaning ("as if never made") for the physics. The explain history between `madeAt` and the start was computed with the change known; re-running from `madeAt` would cost up to an hour of simulation for a change scheduled an hour ahead, so **the re-run still starts at the change's own start** and the forecast cards that were not raised in between are not raised retroactively. After a removal the forecast's swing that is still ahead gets its card at the next cycle; one that has already passed by over 20 minutes gets none. Written in the docs (the first run's cards between `madeAt` and the start are what was said at the time).

**Cards raised before `madeAt`: stay as history.** They are what the engine said when the user decided; retiring them needs a new protocol event and a rewrite of snapshots the history already holds, and the Session's list is append-only from the engine. The Weather tab's one line already says the forecast's later changes no longer apply; the Insights card keeps its own time, so it reads as earlier news. No code.

**Mock engine** (`mockSim.detectInsights`): `known = windDirectionEdits(this.edits).filter(e => (e.madeAt ?? e.time) <= t)` replaces `started`, and the user's card is added (`kind 'wind-change'`, `watch`, same words), raised at `max(madeAt, start - 1800)` if the turn passes the thresholds; no Dead man zone raster there (the demo has none for a forecast swing either beyond the card).

### 1.4 Design (3): "Back to the forecast"

**In the data:** every `windDirection` edit in `state.edits` is removed as if never made, the belt-kit `WindEdit`s stay (they have their own Remove), ignitions and fuel edits stay. `Session.backToForecast(): number` (the number removed): `list = windDirectionEdits(edits)`; none gives 0 and sends nothing; else `controller.removeEdit(id)` for each, **earliest first** (the first restores the checkpoint at or before the earliest start, the later ones are then not applied and only drop their records), `rewindFloor = t_first`, history and cards after `t_first` cut, `edits` without them, `reviewing = false`, one `afterEdit()`, then `controller.run(view)` when paused beyond the head: the body of `removeWindDirection` with `t = t_first`, the filter over all ids. `windWeather()` returns the forecast object again (the existing test "goes back to the forecast object when the last change is removed").
**In the panel** (`windPanel.ts`, `windPanelModel.ts`): under the list `ul`, a button `wind-back-to-forecast` ("Back to the forecast", icon `undo`, `variant: 'secondary'`, the 44 px of `.btn`), and a hint `Back to the forecast takes away every wind change you made, as if you had never made them. The simulation is computed again from the first one.` Shown whenever the list is not empty (with one change it does what the bin does; it is the thing people look for). Announcement (`ctx.announce`, no toast, no `role=alert`): "Back to the forecast. 2 wind changes removed." (one: "1 wind change removed"), plus " The simulation re-runs from 14:40 without them." only when `headTime` is after the first start (`removeAnnouncement`'s rule). **Focus:** after the press the button is gone, so focus goes to the dial (not `keepFocus`, which would land on a belt-kit reading's Remove). **Layout** (windPanelModel rules): the body scrolls and Apply is the fixed foot, so a button at the end of the list changes neither; at 360 x 640 it is reached by scrolling the body, never covered by the foot; every control at least 44 x 44; no horizontal scroll.

## 2. Item F: the eight loose ends

### 2.1 Side view's arrows are short (F1)
**Cause.** `arrowLength(speed, longest)` (`slicePanelModel.ts:399`) is 55 % of `longest` in a calm and `longest = 0.6 * min(cell w, cell h)` of the 24 x 21 px lattice that doubled the density, so a calm arrow is 7.8 to 8.7 px (portrait) and 6.5 to 6.9 px (landscape), 29 to 42 % under 0.5.0's. Only the reduced-motion lattice draws arrows (`slicePanel.ts:264`). **Fix:** `const ARROW_MIN_PX = 8` and `arrowLength = max(min(ARROW_MIN_PX, longest), longest * (0.55 + 0.45 * share))`: the floor never exceeds what the cell holds, so the 40 %-clear rule of the lattice stands (8 of a 21 px cell is 38 %). Portrait calm arrows change by at most 0.7 px, landscape ones by 1.1 to 1.5 px, strong arrows not at all. **Files:** `slicePanelModel.ts`, `slicePanelModel.test.ts` (`arrowLength(0, 13)` was `0.55 * 13`, now 8: updated, with `(0, 15.8)` still 8.69, `(0, 12.5)` 8, `(0, 5)` 5, `(11.4, 13)` 13). **Risk:** low. The comment in `sliceDraw.ts:286` ("about 7 to 13 px") is updated.

### 2.2 The 3-D curtain's arrows bunch edge-on (F2)
**Cause.** `CrossSectionLayer.drawArrows` (`crossSectionLayer.ts:208`) places 21 columns by nine heights by world metres, each arrow `0.85 * spacing * min(1, 0.25 + sp/14)` long along the section line. The `StreakBatch` draws a head of a fixed 10 px (times dpr) and a 2.4 px stroke. When the camera looks along the line, the 21 columns land on one screen column: `f = sqrt(1 - (forward . d)^2)` (1 face-on or from above, 0 looking along the line) shrinks both the column spacing and the arrow length by `f`, so the heads stack. Even face-on at 360 px a full-width curtain has a 15 px column spacing, so the 0.6.0 density is already at the limit there; nothing to change face-on. The arrows are built only when the slice changes, not per frame.
**Fix (display only):** keep the full arrow set in scratch copies (`fullA/B/C`) and commit a strided subset to the batch and its halo when the camera's view changes. `arrowStride(spacingM, f, pxPerM)` (pure, in a new `src/render/curtainArrows.ts`): `px = spacingM * f * pxPerM`, `stride = clamp(ceil(ARROW_MIN_SPACING_PX / px), 1, 7)` with `ARROW_MIN_SPACING_PX = 14` (about the head and a bit of shaft), 15 % hysteresis on `px` so a small camera move cannot flip it, `pxPerM = viewportH / (2 tan(fov/2) * distance to the curtain's middle)`. Every `stride`-th column is drawn (column 0 first, the same ones at each stride). `CrossSectionLayer.setView(camera, viewportH)` is called from the frame loop next to `pollSlice()` (two lines in `SceneView.ts`), costs a few multiplications, and recommits (a copy of at most 189 arrows, 10 floats each) only when the stride changes. **Numbers [est]:** at 360 x 640 with the curtain across the screen (spacing 15.4 px face-on) the stride is 1 at `f` over 0.93, 2 from 0.46, 3 from 0.31, 4 from 0.23, up to 7 (3 columns) edge-on. **The constants need one look at a screenshot** (I could not run a browser): the lane tunes `ARROW_MIN_SPACING_PX` at 360 x 640 and 412 x 915 on the fast and the standard tier. **Files:** `crossSectionLayer.ts`, new `curtainArrows.ts` and `curtainArrows.test.ts`, `crossSectionLayer.test.ts`, two lines of `SceneView.ts`. **Risk:** the recommit when the stride flips (hysteresis tested), the halo batch must get the same subset.

### 2.3 The 844 x 390 tap miss (F3)
**What the report is.** 19.4 and 20.4 record the real-engine run of `scripts/audit-tap-targets.mjs` at 844 x 390 with a recording: "3 of 8 bottom probes missed for every control below the picture, Turn off the slice included"; 21.4 calls it the compass button's. **Cause (by arithmetic, not run).** In short landscape the tool body scrolls between a pinned picture (92 px: 8 padding plus the 84 px canvas) and a pinned footer (`position: sticky; bottom: 0`). `slice.css:147` reserves `scroll-padding: 96px 0 30px`, which is the picture and a **one-line** footer (about 26 px: 17 px of text, 8 px padding, 1 px rule). With a recording the footer wraps to two lines, about 43 px. `scrollIntoView` aligns to the middle of the region left by the scroll-padding, [96, H - 30] for a body of visible height H, so a control's centre lands at `(H + 66)/2`; the footer really starts at `H - 43`. A real finger is also affected: the free band between the pinned parts is H - 43 - 92 = 45 px at H = 180, barely more than one 44 px target.
**Fix:** `scroll-padding: 96px 0 48px` in the landscape rule (same number of characters, `slice.css` stays at 4,066 bytes of its 4,096). Then the region is [96, H - 48], the control's centre `(H + 48)/2`, and the probes clear the footer for H of 180 and up (+21 gives 135 under the footer top at 137). If a real-engine audit still reports misses at the smallest body, shorten the footer to one line in this rule instead (the air kind and the grid, "air from" in the title attribute): owner question 10. **Test:** in `e2e/air-slice.spec.ts`, 844 x 390 with a recording, the `reach`/`onTop` pattern of line 740 extended to "every control below the picture, after a centred scroll, has all eight probes on itself" (the audit's own logic inside the spec). **Files:** `slice.css`, `e2e/air-slice.spec.ts`. **Risk:** low; one more look at `scripts/audit-tap-targets.mjs` with the real engine, under the heavy lock, to confirm (the lane has the numbers to compare).

### 2.6 The two-finger turn without OrbitControls' private members (F6)
**What `TwoFingerTurnControls` overrides today** (`cameraRig.ts:93-109`): `_rotateLeft(angle)` (three r186: `this._sphericalDelta.theta -= angle`, line 953) to call it with `-angle` while a two-finger move is handled, and `_handleTouchMoveDollyRotate(event)` (line 1447: `if (enableZoom) _handleTouchMoveDolly(event); if (enableRotate) _handleTouchMoveRotate(event);`) only to raise a flag around the stock call. `_handleTouchMoveRotate` (line 1361) takes the midpoint of the event's pointer and the other pointer's stored position, `_rotateDelta = (end - start) * rotateSpeed`, calls `_rotateLeft(2 pi * dx / clientHeight)` and `_rotateUp(2 pi * dy / clientHeight)` (`_sphericalDelta.phi -= angle`), and advances `_rotateStart`. `update()` (697) adds `delta * dampingFactor` to theta and phi (or the whole delta without damping), clamps phi to `[minPolarAngle, maxPolarAngle]` and `makeSafe()`s it, applies pan, dolly and zoom-to-cursor, then multiplies the deltas by `1 - dampingFactor`. `onTouchMove` calls `update()` after every touch move. The dolly half (pinch, `_updateZoomParameters` for zoom to the cursor) must stay stock.

**Design (public API only).** A class `TwoFingerTurn` in `src/render/twoFingerTurn.ts`, built before `OrbitControls` like `PanGesture` (capture listeners on the same element, so they run first and a third finger's `stopImmediatePropagation` by `PanGesture` hides it from them too):
1. Tracks `touch` pointers only (id to clientX/Y). On the second finger's `pointerdown` in an owned mode (`orbit`, `top`: the rig says so) with `controls.enabled` and `accepts()`: `startMid = mean`, `active = true`, `wasRotate = controls.enableRotate`, `controls.enableRotate = false`. That makes the stock DOLLY_ROTATE handlers do the pinch and skip the turn (`touches.TWO` stays `DOLLY_ROTATE`, so the structure test at line 442 stands).
2. On each `pointermove` of a tracked finger while active: update the position, `mid = mean`, `d = mid - startMid`, `startMid = mid`, and accumulate `theta += 2 pi * rotateSpeed * d.x / clientHeight * SIGN`, `phi -= 2 pi * rotateSpeed * d.y / clientHeight` with `SIGN = -1` for the mirrored turn (stock `_rotateLeft` does `-= angle`; ours is `+= angle`, hence the anticlockwise drag to the right). Same event order as stock: ours runs first, then OrbitControls' `onTouchMove` handles the pinch and calls `update()`.
3. `TwoFingerTurnControls.update()` (public, overridden): before `super.update()` apply one damped step: `f = enableDamping ? dampingFactor : 1`; `stepTheta = theta * f; stepPhi = phi * f; theta *= 1 - f; phi *= 1 - f` (without damping both go to zero); rotate the camera about `target` by those steps in spherical terms: `offset = position - target`, `Spherical.setFromVector3`, `theta += stepTheta`, `phi = clamp(phi + stepPhi, minPolarAngle, maxPolarAngle)`, back to a vector, `position = target + offset`. `super.update()` then reads the turned position, adds its own mouse and pinch work, clamps again (a no-op) and `lookAt`s. This runs for every `update()` call, so the damping decays per call exactly as stock's (including the extra calls from touch events), and goes on after the fingers lift (inertia).
4. On the next `pointerup`/`pointercancel`/`lostpointercapture` of a tracked finger, a mode change, `setInteractionEnabled(false)`, a flight and `dispose`: `active = false`, `controls.enableRotate = wasRotate`. Accumulated damping is left to run out (stock leaves `_sphericalDelta` the same way). `ground` mode: not owned, `touches.TWO = ROTATE` stock, nothing here runs (eye level as now). The mouse never goes through this (its rotate is stock, `enableRotate` is true then).
**What stays identical:** the sign and speed (100 px is 30 degrees at `rotateSpeed` 2/3 on an 800 px element), damping and its per-call decay, the polar limits (orbit 0 to 86 degrees, top 0 to 0.001 so the turn only), pinch and zoom to the cursor (stock), one-finger pan (`PanGesture`), eye level, the mouse. **What differs numerically:** the turn is applied to Cartesian then re-read as spherical (1e-15 relative), not bitwise; the 54 tests compare with tolerances (to be confirmed by running them, `toBeCloseTo` in the file) and the verifier's frame-by-frame check against 0.5.0 gets a tolerance of 1e-9 rad.
**Existing tests that pin it:** `cameraRig.gestures.test.ts` (54: the whole "two fingers turn, tilt and zoom" and "which way the two-finger turn goes" groups, `maps to OrbitControls DOLLY_ROTATE`, "switching between one and two fingers", "cancel, interaction off, flights", "eye level, mouse, pen"), `cameraRig.relief.test.ts`, `groundPan.test.ts`, `src/render/testing/rigHarness.ts` (the fake element), `e2e/gestures.spec.ts` (real multi-touch). **New tests** (added to the gestures file, none changed): the turn survives `three` without `_rotateLeft` and `_handleTouchMoveDollyRotate` (the class no longer reads `OrbitControls.prototype` members that start with `_`: a source check in the test), `enableRotate` is restored after lift, cancel, a third finger, a mode change and a flight, never left false, a two-finger turn then a mouse right-drag still go their own ways (existing test), a damped turn after the fingers lift decays like stock (record 40 frames of the old class from a saved copy of the numbers: 30 degrees per 100 px, then the ratio of successive steps `1 - dampingFactor`).
**Risk:** medium (the one change in this list that touches input). The per-call decay and the event order are the things to get right; the toggle of `enableRotate` is the single stateful trick (see 6).

### 2.7 `flowGlyphs` hash (F7)
**What it is.** `hash(a, b, c)` (`flowGlyphs.ts:274`, a copy of `hash3`, `fields.ts:20`) is `lowbias32(imul(a,K1) ^ imul(b,K2) ^ imul(c,K3))`; the finishing mix is a bijection, so two cells collide exactly when the xor of the products does. **MEASURED** (`hashcol.mjs`, `hashcentre.mjs`, `hashoff.mjs`): in a 1000 x 1000 window centred on the origin 166,620 cells (16.7 %) share their hash with another cell, against about 116 for a good 32-bit hash; 166,537 of those pairs are `(i, j)` and `(-i, -j)`. Away from the origin there is nothing: a 600 x 600 window of positive indices has **0 of 3,321 offsets** within 40 cells that ever collide. **Why:** `imul(-a, K) = -(a*K)`, and `(-u) xor (-v) = u xor v` whenever `u` and `v` have the same number of trailing zeros, which for odd `K` means `a` and `b` do: one cell in three has a twin through the origin (the cells 2 apart in x and 10 in y that `detailStreaks.ts:389` mentions are such a pair near the origin, `(1,5)` and `(-1,-5)`). **Can it be seen?** The twin has the same jitter and the same keep or drop for the cap and the horizon thinning, but its seed sits at the point-reflected cell centre plus the *same* offset, not a reflected one, and the two cells are on opposite sides of the picture. No repeated row, no pair of neighbours, no pattern a person can pick out. So **no visible repetition, results unchanged** (D7). **If the owner wants it gone:** use the sum of the products in the pre-mix, as `hashSum` already does in `detailStreaks.ts:393` (collides only 65,000 cells apart); every glyph then moves within its cell (display only) and `flowGlyphs.test.ts`, `flowGlyphs.oracle.test.ts`, `glyphOracle.testing.ts` (which writes the seed with `hash01`) and the e2e counts must be re-baselined together, with the reason in the report; `fields.hash3` (vegetation, smoke, embers) stays. **Files for the default:** a comment on `hash` saying the above (and that `detailStreaks.hash` has it too), one test `flowGlyphs.test.ts` "the cell hash has no twin among neighbours away from the origin" (a 128 x 128 window at (300, 300): 16,384 cells, 0 equal), nothing else.
