# Red team of the option B plan (2026-11-12)

**Target:** `docs/plans/dotnet-option-b-prototype.md` (the staged prototype plan, with its revisions 1 and 2), the artifact "FireSim on .NET" (https://claude.ai/artifact/VsrSVEJaknFbyWuQh7Svx9), and the premise behind both: that running FireSim's solver in C# on .NET 11 is worth testing.

**Goal:** find what would make the plan, its gates or its premise wrong, before anyone spends more on it. The test of success is the number of *true* defects found, not the number of findings.

## 1. What the research says about doing this well

| Finding | Source | What it changed in the design |
|---|---|---|
| Agents that debate each other drift toward agreement; sycophancy and conformity grow with more rounds, and debate can lower accuracy. Debate costs 2 to 3 times the tokens of isolated self-correction for no better accuracy. | [The Cost of Consensus](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00914v1); [peer conformity in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18998) | The red teams are **isolated**: they never see one another's output and there is no debate round. |
| Most of the gain credited to multi-agent debate comes from majority voting, not from the debate. | [NeurIPS 2025 analysis cited in the search](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00914) | Findings are settled by **independent verification and voting**, not discussion. |
| Models are biased by who wrote an answer (identity bias); anonymising helps. | [Identity bias in multi-agent debate](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07517v1) | Verifiers see a **claim and its evidence, not its author or lens**. |
| Imagining that the failure has already happened ("prospective hindsight") raised the number of reasons people generated by about 30 %, in one 1989 study that did not measure whether the reasons were right. | [Premortem overview](https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/premortem) | One team does a **pre-mortem**, and every reason it gives needs evidence and a verifier. Quantity is not accepted as quality. |
| Red teaming works on assumptions: list them, rate how critical each is and how well it is evidenced, stress them with extreme cases, keep the team independent and early, and end every finding in a specific action. A checklist is a scaffold, not a guarantee. | [UK MoD Red Teaming Handbook](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/61702155e90e07197867eb93/20210625-Red_Teaming_Handbook.pdf); [US Army Red Team Handbook](https://newandimproved.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/ufmcs_red_team_handbook_apr2011.pdf) | One team builds an **assumptions register**; every finding must name the **plan change** it implies. |
| Judges from one model family share blind spots; ensembles from different families, answer-first judging, defined tie rules and calibration against known answers reduce false passes. A judge can be fooled if its verdicts are accepted unchecked. | [LLM-as-judge ensembles](https://galileo.ai/blog/why-llm-as-a-judge-fails); [multi-judge calibration](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12002v1); [practitioner guide](https://polyaxon.com/blog/llm-as-a-judge/) | Verifiers come from **two model families**, must **form their own view from sources first**, follow a stated tie rule, and are **calibrated with planted true and false claims**. |

The research is mostly recent preprints and practitioner writing, not settled results; the design takes the direction they agree on.

## 2. The design

**Stage A, seven isolated red teams** (read-only, with web research; none sees another's work):

| Team | Lens | Model |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Platform | Are the Android, MAUI, .NET 11, BlazorWebView and HybridWebView claims true, and can the prototype be built and run as planned? | Sonnet |
| R2 Method | Can a gate pass when B is bad, or stop when B is good? Baselines, noise, thresholds, sample sizes, Amdahl, phones. | Opus |
| R3 Port fidelity | Numerics, random streams, ordering, threads, the size of the port, hidden JavaScript semantics, cross-platform determinism. | Sonnet |
| R4 Status quo | Steelman not doing B: cheaper routes to the same goal, the evidence behind the 2 to 2.6 times, what the owner actually gains. | Opus |
| R5 Operations | Security, stores, privacy, supply chain, saved-data continuity, lifecycle, size, battery. | Sonnet |
| R6 Pre-mortem | It is six months later and the effort failed or was regretted: why? Plus the assumptions register. | Sonnet |
| R7 Blind | Given only the question, never the plan: design its own prototype plan and failure list. The gaps against our plan are the findings. | Opus |

Each team returns at most eight findings in a fixed format: a falsifiable claim, evidence (URL with date and quote, file and line, or command and output), the plan change it implies, severity, confidence, and what the team checked and found sound. "No finding" is acceptable; padding is not.

**Stage B, verification.** The orchestrator merges duplicates and removes author and lens. Each finding goes to two verifiers, one Sonnet and one Opus, who first research the question themselves and then judge: CONFIRMED, PARTLY, REFUTED or CANNOT TELL, with a severity. A finding stands when both confirm or one confirms and one says partly. Split verdicts are reported as unresolved, with both reasons, not decided by me. Two planted claims, one clearly true and one clearly false, are mixed into the batches unlabelled; a verifier that confirms the false one is discounted.

**Stage C, synthesis.** The orchestrator lists confirmed findings, the plan changes they imply (gates, stages, thresholds, scope), what remains unresolved, and what the red teams found sound. R7's plan is compared with ours for gaps. The plan is revised and the artifact updated.

## 3. Rules for every team
Read-only on the repository (read the plan, the artifact file, the reports and the app source; edit nothing; no pushes). Write the final report to `<scratchpad>/redteam/<team>.md` as well as returning it. Heavy work is not needed. Evidence is primary (Microsoft Learn, the dotnet/maui, dotnet/aspnetcore and dotnet/runtime repositories, Android developer documentation, Chromium documentation, papers); secondary sources only to find primary ones. Inference is labelled as inference.

### 4.2 Calibration

All ten verifiers confirmed the planted true claim and refuted the planted false one: 20 judgements out of 20. That tells us less than it seems. The false control could be refuted by opening `src/core/rng.ts`, and the true one sat on Microsoft's own page.

A better test of discrimination came from the real items. Inside claims that the red teams had stated as fact, the verifiers found several errors:
- BlazorWebView's default origin was given the wrong way round.
- The ember share was given as 25.6 % where 12.8 % is right, and with it a speed-up cap of 2.2 times where about 1.3 times is right.
- The claim that "Mono is no longer an option" is false for the GA packs.
- A disk figure did not appear in the logs it cited.
- A noise result at 1800 s did not reproduce.
- The briefs themselves quoted a Gate 2 rate (6.5 MB at 2 per second) that is not in the plan.

**Tally.**
- Both verifiers confirmed 18 findings. One confirmed and the other said partly on 8 more, so by the tie rule 26 findings stand.
- Both said partly on 9. The tie rule did not cover this case. I treated each such finding as standing in the narrowed form both verifiers agree on, and recorded what was narrowed (see 4.4).
- None was refuted and none was "cannot tell".
- The two verifiers gave the same verdict on 27 of 35 and the same severity on 26 of 35.
- Where they differed, Opus was the stricter on 5 items (G-1, T-1, T-4, T-5, S-7) and Sonnet on 3 (P-6, P-8, N-7).

### 4.4 What did not work as designed

- **The tie rule had a hole.** "Both partly" was not defined, and 9 of 35 findings landed there. A rule should cover every pair of verdicts.
- **Answer-first was on trust.** Each batch file held the questions and the claims together. Three Opus verifiers said so in their reports. The answer-first step should be enforced: send the question, collect the answer, then send the claim.
- **Isolation leaked in places.**
  - Every team got its own git worktree, but the scratchpad was shared. R1, R3 and R5 read output from the stopped build agents.
  - R6 opened the first lines of this design document.
  - No team read another team's report.
- **The briefs carried my own errors.**
  - R3's brief stated as fact that V8 computes Math functions the same way on every device. That stopped being true with V8 15. R3 and verifier N-2 corrected it.
  - R2's brief and batch G quoted a Gate 2 rate that is not in the plan.
- **Severity was inflated at the source.** The teams filed 8 BLOCKERs. After verification only three items carry a blocker rating, and each from one verifier: G-5, G-9, and N-9 for stage 4 only.
- **More teams agreeing did not mean a cleaner finding.** Of the four findings raised by four teams, both verifiers confirmed only one, because merging four reports gives a composite claim with more places to be overstated. Of the twelve single-team findings, both verifiers confirmed eight, because they were narrow.

### 4.5 Where the teams disagreed with each other

- **Last-bit maths.**
  - R2 and R4 listed revision 2's statement ("last-bit math differences are not the risk") as sound.
  - R3, R5, R6 and R7 attacked it.
  - Both verifiers re-ran the noise injection. The 1e-15 evidence was vacuous for the fire and the air, while the conclusion held at the bars tested. Each side was half right.
- **Mono.**
  - R2 and R3, through secondary sources, said Mono was removed in Preview 6.
  - Verifier S-O found the GA packs still map `UseMonoRuntime=true` to MonoVM. I confirmed this in the installed .NET for Android and MAUI 11.0.0 targets.
  - Microsoft Learn says Mono is not supported for MAUI apps that target .NET 11. Not settled.
- **BlazorWebView origin.** R1 read `0.0.0.0` out of the DLL. T-O traced the default to `0.0.0.1`, with a switch for `0.0.0.0`.
- **Port size.** R4 and R6 found the 25,000-line figure sound for the solver folders. R3 and R7 counted the import closure. Both counts are right; they measure different things.

### 4.6 The blind team (R7) against our plan

R7 never saw the plan. Its own plan arrived independently at:
- a stage 0 premise check on the owner's phone;
- the frozen K1 kernel as the first C# test;
- one APK that runs both engines in A B B A order, with thermal logging;
- binary bridges (an intercepted fetch, ArrayBuffer messages);
- the whole step instead of kernels;
- the two-solver question;
- port size including explain.

Each of these was a gap in our plan and was also found by at least one lens team.

R7 had three things no other team had:
- bit-identity as the correctness gate, by porting V8's fdlibm into C#;
- measuring the C# kernels as Blazor WebAssembly with AOT, to price the "two solvers" answer;
- measuring the port rate and extrapolating the total, with a solver freeze during the slice.

Our plan, unlike R7's, covers:
- host security and the phone's loopback threat;
- the signing key and saved-data continuity;
- Capacitor's plugins;
- target SDK 37;
- lifecycle;
- toolchain provenance;
- the same-tier yardstick from 3a.

R7 made one factual error: it said .NET 11 had not been released. It was released on 2026-11-10.

Adopted from R7 in revision 3:
- stage 0;
- the K1 checksum;
- the one-APK A B B A protocol;
- the routes (d) and (e);
- the `FsMath` routing point, which keeps the fdlibm port open.

Not adopted yet:
- the WebAssembly arm. The owner's answer to 0a.2 decides whether it is needed.
- the port-rate measurement. It belongs to stage 3.
