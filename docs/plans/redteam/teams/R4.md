# R4: the status quo review of the option B plan (2026-11-12)

Lens: make the strongest case for NOT doing option B. Is the premise sound, and is there a cheaper way to get what the owner wants?

F1. Gate 1 compares C# on all cores with TypeScript on one core. TypeScript threads could pass it, so it cannot show that C# is worth having.
Severity: BLOCKER   Confidence: HIGH   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: Stage 1 passes if "all-core C#" is at least 1.5 times faster than "the TypeScript in Node" on desktop (2.0 times on the phone). The TypeScript side runs on one thread. Plan 15e's own Amdahl bounds give 1.8 to 2.6 times from threads alone on 4 cores. D34 already allows those threads in TypeScript. So Gate 1 can pass even if C# is no faster than V8 per core, and the gain it measures is one the status quo can also get.
Evidence: docs/plans/dotnet-option-b-prototype.md:34 "All-core C# is under 1.5× the TypeScript on desktop, or under 2.0× on the phone"; :40 "Port the chosen kernel three ways (scalar, Span<T> with Vector<T>, Parallel.For with a fixed partition), and benchmark each against the TypeScript in Node". docs/plans/air-slice-wind-detail-honest-speed.md:1018 "Amdahl upper bounds on 4 cores: the air alone 1.9x (Standard) and 2.6x (High); the fire alone 1.8 to 2.0x". D34 (line 121): "Speed beyond one core comes from threads, and from WebAssembly or WebGPU for one hot loop only if the measurement says so, within the TypeScript project". For scale on one thread, see the Benchmarks Game, Node v23.8.0 against .NET 9.0.100, undated release 25.03, fetched 2026-11-12 (https://benchmarksgame-team.pages.debian.net/benchmarksgame/fastest/node-csharpcore.html): spectral-norm CPU 3.44 s (C#) against 6.03 s (Node), n-body 3.18 s against 8.60 s. The C# entries are flagged for hand-written SIMD or unsafe code, so plain per-core gains of about 1.5 to 2.7 times are plausible, not certain. These numbers predate .NET 11.
Why it matters: A pass would be read as "there is a speed argument for B". Gate 1 then gates the expensive stage 3. But the part of the gain that comes from threads needs no port and no second solver.
Plan change: Split Gate 1 in two. (a) Per core: C# with 1 thread against TypeScript with 1 thread, same kernel, same data. (b) The B-specific gain: C# on all cores against a TypeScript split of the same kernel over the same number of Node worker_threads, or at least against the 15e Amdahl bound applied to the TypeScript. Gate on (b): B must beat TypeScript with threads by the stated margin, not beat TypeScript on one thread. 15e step 3 ("a small spike of option 2 or 3 and a measured speed-up over one core") was never done. It is the missing baseline, and it costs far less than K1.
Already covered?: no. Section 7 says "The Node host already gives the desktop its speed", but the Gate 1 baseline ignores the Node host's threads.

F2. The plan does not say which user-visible limit a faster solver would remove, and B's best case removes none that is known.
Severity: MAJOR   Confidence: MEDIUM   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: On the one phone measured, the 3-D step takes 41 ms against Auto's cut-off of 198 ms (4.8 times headroom). On desktop, one thread already runs a 10 m fast-tier run of 4 hours in about 3 minutes. The playback speeds are 1, 60, 600, 3600 times real time and as fast as possible. My arithmetic (inference): the phone's 3-D tier runs at about 175 to 240 times real time and its fast tier at about 290 times. A 2 to 2.6 times gain puts both at about 450 to 600 times. That is still at or below the 600 times step and far below 3600 times. Option B's best case, as 15e bounds it, changes no tier choice and crosses no speed step.
Evidence: docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:424-427 "the cut-off is a step of 198.4 ms ... The one phone measured so far took 41 ms ... the new cut-off is 4.8 times that step". docs/plans/air-slice-wind-detail-honest-speed.md:1016 "One thread already does 10 m ... 4 h at 10 m on 6 km takes about 3 minutes", and :1018 "the speed the desktop already has is more than a 10 m run needs". src/ui/session.chaos.test.ts:111 speeds `[1, 60, 600, 3600, Infinity]`. The 290 times figure is the desktop 30 m fast tier (679 times, line 1016) divided by the measured phone/desktop ratio of 2.35 (line 36). That is inference.
Why it matters: Without a named benefit, Gate 1 can pass on a speed-up that no user sees, and the owner would be asked to reopen D34 for it.
Plan change: Add a premise check before stage 1. Name the user-visible outcome that B must change. Examples: "the 3-D tier keeps up at 600× on the owner's phone", "a 12 h run at 10 m on the desktop finishes in under N minutes", or "Auto picks the 3-D tier on a phone that today gets Fast". Get the owner's agreement on it. Then make the phone half of Gate 1 "the measured C# rate crosses that line and TypeScript with workers does not", not a bare ratio.
Already covered?: no. Section 7, "Gate 1 fails: no speed argument", treats any ratio of 1.5 or more as an argument.

F3. Cheaper routes to the same compute are now available within D34, and the plan does not compare them.
Severity: MAJOR   Confidence: MEDIUM   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: As of 2026, the TypeScript project can get more compute on the phone without a port, through four routes:
- androidx.webkit 1.18.0 (2026-10-21) adds a profile-level cross-origin-isolated allowlist. It enables SharedArrayBuffer, and with it WebAssembly threads, inside Android WebView.
- Fixed-width WebAssembly SIMD has followed Chrome in WebView since Chrome 91 and needs no SharedArrayBuffer.
- WebGPU is reported to have shipped in WebView around Chrome 146.
- Plain Web Workers with transferred buffers already work in the app.
There is also an algorithmic route. The 15c desk analysis estimates that refining to 10 m only in a moving band of complex cells near the front costs 2.7 times today's 30 m, against 27 times for a uniform 10 m grid. That ten-fold saving is far more than B's 2 to 2.6 times.
Evidence: https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/webkit (fetched 2026-11-12), version 1.18.0 of October 21, 2026: "new profile-level APIs (Profile#setCrossOriginIsolatedAllowlist ...) to allow the use of SharedArrayBuffer ... Web pages must also opt in by setting the Document-Isolation-Policy header". MDN browser-compat-data, wasm fixed-width SIMD: WebView Android mirrors Chrome 91 (via search summary; secondary). Chromium blink-dev "Intent to Ship: WebGPU Compatibility mode" (January 2026) gives WebView shipping in 146 as an estimate. I did not confirm the shipped version. docs/plans/air-slice-wind-detail-honest-speed.md:948 "the moving band 1.2 and 2.7 times" against "uniform 10 m 27 times" (an estimate assuming equal cost per cell-step).
Caveats: the allowlist depends on the installed WebView version. Whether Capacitor's Android local server can send the Document-Isolation-Policy header is unverified: an Ionic forum thread asking about custom headers has no accepted answer.
Why it matters: B is the most expensive way to buy at most 2 to 2.6 times. The plan never asks whether the same gain is reachable without retiring a solver.
Plan change: Add a "status quo arm" stage of about one agent run, before K1 or beside it. (1) Split the 3-D air step over 2 to 4 Web Workers in a Capacitor test build, using the allowlist plus Document-Isolation-Policy, with transferred halos as the fallback. (2) Check that `crossOriginIsolated` is true on the owner's phone. (3) Report the speed-up next to K1's. Record the 15c moving-band estimate in section 7 as the algorithmic alternative.
Already covered?: no. Neither the plan nor the artifact mentions androidx.webkit 1.18. 15e predates it (2026-10-31 desk note).

F4. On the phone, "Parallel.For with a fixed partition" is the worst way to schedule work across big and little cores. Gate 1's phone number would say more about the scheduling than about C#.
Severity: MAJOR   Confidence: MEDIUM   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: Phones with 6 to 8 cores mix 1 to 4 big cores with slower efficiency cores. If the work is split into equal pieces with a fixed assignment to threads, every step waits for the slowest core. The all-core result can even be worse than running on the big cores only. Determinism (rule 4) needs fixed chunk boundaries and a fixed reduction order. It does not need a fixed thread assignment. The plan blurs the two, so the phone half of Gate 1 can fail for a reason that has nothing to do with C#.
Evidence: plan :40 "Parallel.For with a fixed partition"; plan :12 "identical results on 1, 2, 4 and 8 threads". Android developers, "Analyze thread scheduling" (https://developer.android.com/agi/sys-trace/threads-scheduling, current page, read through a search summary): core affinity has been replaced by the Performance Hint API (ADPF) on Android 12 and later, core classes are "by common convention", and migration has costs. TVM forum on the Snapdragon 835 (2017, secondary anecdote): an even static split over big and little cores fell below big-cores-only. There is no 2026 primary benchmark of mid-range scaling; the expected 6 to 8 core gain of about 1.5 to 2.5 times for the parallel part is inference.
Why it matters: A false failure stops B. A false pass gets extrapolated from one flagship phone.
Plan change: Partition into many fixed chunks (more than the core count), assign them dynamically, and reduce in a fixed order. Record the SoC core topology and the per-thread-count curve (1, 2, 4, all). Run a sustained thermal test of 10 minutes, not a short benchmark. Ask for a mid-range phone, as section 5 already prefers. Apply the same rule to the TypeScript worker arm in F3.
Already covered?: partly. Section 5 asks for a mid-range phone. Nothing covers big/little scheduling or heat.

F5. B cannot retire the TypeScript solver while the app still runs in a browser, so B means two solvers for good. That is exactly what D34 rejected.
Severity: MAJOR   Confidence: HIGH   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: The app runs in a desktop browser and as a PWA with the worker, in-thread and demo engines. The validation suite and the benchmarks run the TypeScript `Simulation` headless in Node. A C# solver in a MAUI or ASP.NET process cannot serve any of that, and Blazor WebAssembly in .NET 11 still runs on Mono with no real threads (according to the artifact). So under B the TypeScript solver either stays as a second solver for good, or the browser/PWA build and the Node validation tooling are dropped. Section 7's "remaining cost" list mentions neither.
Evidence: air-slice-wind-detail-honest-speed.md:1022 "the same Simulation runs headless in Node (the validation suite and the benchmarks do) ... The app already runs in a desktop browser and as a PWA". :1009 "the worker, in-thread and demo engines". D34 (line 121): ".NET idea ... set aside because it means maintaining two solvers". The artifact (firesim-dotnet.html): "B ... A second solver in practice", and "Blazor WebAssembly ... in .NET 11 it still runs on the Mono runtime with no real threads". Solver size measured here: src/{fire,embers,atmosphere,sim,terrain,core} hold 27,828 non-test lines and 15,210 test lines (`find ... | xargs cat | wc -l`).
Why it matters: The owner would be judging "port 25,000 lines once" when the real choice is "maintain two solvers indefinitely, or drop the web build".
Plan change: Add to section 7, "All pass", a required decision before any further port: which deployments keep the TypeScript solver (browser, PWA, Node validation, e2e). If any do, state the yearly cost of keeping the two in agreement, for example a cross-engine comparison in every gate run (15d idea 19). If none do, state what is lost.
Already covered?: no. Section 7 names the port, the Razor screens and the migration, but not permanent duplication.

F6. "All pass, then D34 is reopened" treats technical feasibility as a reason to reopen. The plan never says what evidence would justify reopening D34.
Severity: MAJOR   Confidence: HIGH   Lens: R4 status quo
Claim: D34 was decided on cost (maintaining two solvers), not on feasibility. None of gates 1 to 5 measures that cost or a benefit that would outweigh it. So an "all pass" result gives no evidence on the question D34 settled, yet section 7 presents it as the trigger for reopening.
Evidence: plan :93 "All pass: B is technically feasible ... D34 would then be reopened by the owner". D34 text: "weighed with you and set aside because it means maintaining two solvers". D39 (line 126): "Threads, WebAssembly and WebGPU are weighed against the 15e measurement before anything is built (it says about 2 to 2.6 times on four cores at best, and D34 says one solver)".
Why it matters: The prototype's spend creates momentum to reverse a decision without evidence on what that decision was about.
Plan change: Add a section, "Evidence needed to reopen D34", that all of the following must meet:
- (i) the user-visible benefit from F2 is achieved;
- (ii) B beats TypeScript with workers, WebAssembly or WebGPU (F1, F3) by a stated margin on a mid-range phone;
- (iii) the two-solver or dropped-web question from F5 is answered;
- (iv) the cost of rerunning validation on a new baseline is estimated, including the 30-seed ensembles that section 10 still lists as not run;
- (v) the cost is weighed against the 0.9.0 and 0.10.0 work (F8).
Reword section 7 to "All pass: B is feasible; whether D34 is reopened depends on (i) to (v)".
Already covered?: no.

Why it matters: The owner may come away believing ".NET for this app" stands or falls on the solver port, when a shell-only route (A without Razor) needs no port and leaves D34 alone.

## Checked and found sound
- Solver and test line counts. The plan's "about 25,000 lines and 15,000 lines of tests" matches what I measured: 27,828 non-test and 15,210 test lines in the six solver folders.
- The 15e evidence is quoted correctly in D39 ("about 2 to 2.6 times on four cores at best"). Those are Amdahl estimates, not measurements, and 15e itself says so (line 1020, "Estimated: the Amdahl bounds").
- Section 7 already stops B when Gate 1 fails, with the right reason ("The Node host already gives the desktop its speed").
- Section 10's correction is supported by 3a-reference.md and 3a-tier-difference.md: last-bit math differences are not the risk, the ember random stream is.
- Stage 5 already names the origin and saved-data migration risk and the API 24 floor. Microsoft Learn's .NET MAUI 11 page (ms.date 2026-09-10) confirms API 24, CoreCLR as the default, and NativeAOT as experimental and opt-in on Android.
- D34 is stated as still in force in plan :5, and the prototype is kept out of src/ (rule 1). It does not quietly reopen D34 in code. The reopening problem is in the framing of section 7 (F6).

## Could not check
