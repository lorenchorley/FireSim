## 1. How it is installed (no edit of `src/`)

- **Module functions** (89): `plugin.mjs` is a Vite plugin in the manner of `pow-lowering-plugin.mjs`. After TypeScript is stripped it renames each named top-level function `name__fsp` and adds a wrapper with the original name and the original `export`, so every caller goes through it. The wrapper calls straight through until `install.ts` sets `globalThis.__FSP` (so the scenario builder, which runs before the solver, is not recorded).
- **Methods** (138): `install.ts` replaces `Class.prototype[method]` with a wrapper (`TerrainFeaturesImpl`, which the module does not export, is patched from `sim.features` when the first `Simulation` is hooked). Constructors cannot be wrapped: their inputs are reached through the first calls that use them (`buildAtmosGrid`, `EllipticSolver.build`).
- **Callbacks the embers receive** (`windFn`, `turbFn`, `landingFn`, `igniteFn`, arrow fields of `Simulation`): the instance fields are replaced by recording wrappers when the simulation is first stepped. The ember model compares none of them by identity.
- **Observation only.** A wrapper calls the original with the same `this` and arguments and returns its result. Before and after a recorded call it only reads (hashes, or copies typed arrays for a raw frame). It draws no random number and writes no solver state. `--probes` builds into `fixtures/.build-ts-export-probes`; a build without `--probes` is as it always was.
- **Windows** (a step belongs to the window its start time is in): `init` (the constructor and the t0 snapshot), `spin` (`spinUp`, the 900 s of 3-D air of the standard tier), `w0` (steps starting before 120 s), `w600` (595 to 625 s, the 600 s moisture update), `w900` (880 to 960 s). Outside the windows a probe only counts its calls. `spec.mjs` says per probe how many calls of a window are recorded (`first`, then every `stride`-th up to `max`) and how many get a raw frame (`raw`).

## 2. The four probes

### 2.1 Environment tape (`env-tape.json`, `blobs.bin`)

One entry per `FireSpreadModel.prepare` (once per air step, before the fire step), for the whole run: `k` (step index), `t`, `dt`, `tier`, `stampIdx`, the scalars of `SpreadEnvironment` (`droughtFactor`, `kbdi`, `time`, `sunElevation`, `lmstHour`, `cloudFrac`, `mountainPhenomena`, `pyrogenicOn`, `coupling`), the weather hour in force (`weather`, every field), the stable-night state after the step (`night`), and the three arrays the moisture model gives the fire: `moisture`, `availability`, `fuelTempC` (`{$ta,n,h}` plus `blob` where the array is kept).
**The host feeds nothing else per step in these scenarios.** `hostCalls` in the file lists the whole drive: `new Simulation`, `spinUp(Infinity)`, `advance(runTo, Infinity)`, `emitRunEnd`. `host.ts` also has `setOption`, `setQuality`, edits and rewind; no gate scenario sends one (`edits: []`), and `advance` never clips a step to `until`, so how the host chunks the run cannot change the physics. Everything that varies per step (ambient hour, `setAmbient`, `setTime`, stable night, sun) is computed inside `Simulation.stepOnce` from the weather series in `init.bin`: the recorders of A14/A15 hold those calls and `index.json` `callSequence` holds their order.
Arrays are kept in `blobs.bin` (deduplicated by content) for **every step of the 3 km runs** (fast, standard and their `-noembers` twins: 6 and 37 MB), and for steps before 600 s and in `w600` and `w900` in the `-4h` runs (19 to 32 MB); elsewhere only the hash is kept.

### 2.2 Air tape (`air-tape.json`, `blobs.bin`, `ember-*.f64`)

- **Air to fire**, one entry per `prepare`: `windU`, `windV` (the fire's wind), `windBgU`, `windBgV` (background 10 m wind), `fireIndU`, `fireIndV` (fire-induced wind), `uRidge`, `airT`, `airRho`, each `{$ta,n,h[,blob]}`. With these a fire area can be run with no air at all (the fire reads nothing else from the air).
- **Air to embers** (windows `w0` and `w900` only): every `atm.sample` (wind: `x, y, zAGL, u, v, w`) and `atm.sampleTurb` (`x, y, zAGL, zi, wStar, uStar`) call made inside `EmberModel.step`, as float64 records in call order (`ember-wind-w0.f64` and so on; fast: 6,156 wind records in `w0` and 17,467 in `w900`, standard 14,863 and 27,528; capped at 300,000 per log, `truncated` says if it was). Replaying by call order is exact as long as the C# ember model calls with the same arguments in the same order: a mismatch of an argument is the first sign of a wrong particle.
- **Fire to embers**, the landing answers (`landingFn(x, y)` returns fuel, moisture, distance to the front, front ROS...) and the `igniteFn` calls, as lists of exact values in `air-tape.json` (`landing`, `ignite`, up to 20,000 and 5,000 per window). `steps.json` `emberLog` gives which entries of each log belong to which step.

### 2.3 Call recorders (`calls/A<n>.json`, `raw/*.bin`)

For each probe and window, up to `first`/`stride`/`max` calls. A record: `{i, t, a, z?, r?, s?, raw?}`: `i` the call's index in the window, `t` the step start, `a` the descriptors of the arguments before the call, `z` the descriptors of the arguments that the call changed (out-parameters), `r` the result, `s` `{b, a}` the state of `this` before and after (a named list of fields or a digest), `raw` the file of a raw frame. Descriptors keep every scalar exactly (JSON number, or `{"$num":"NaN|Infinity|-Infinity|-0"}`), so **a function of scalars, or of scalar records, can be replayed from the JSON alone** (A5: `headRosKernel`, `ellipseSpeeds`, `hybridHead`...; A1 `weatherAt`; A12 `plumeColumn`); arrays are `{$ta,n,h}` (FNV-1a 32 of their bytes). A raw frame is a codec frame (the same as `init.bin`), `{probe, window, i, t, before:{args, state}, after:{args, ret, state}}`, so any call with a raw frame can be replayed in isolation: the before side holds the arguments **and the `this` state the call reads** (copied before the call), the after side what it left. Capped: a frame over `rawMaxBytes` of arrays is skipped (`rawSkipped`), and a run stops writing frames at 300 MB (`FS_PROBES_RAW_MB`; `FS_PROBES_RAW=0` writes none).
The committed golden of each area is, per probe and window: the call count, the recorded count, `chain` (FNV-1a of the JSON of all its recorded records, so a regeneration is compared in one number) and the first two records in full.

### 2.4 Golden draws (`draws.json`, `goldens/generators.json.gz`)

- **Standalone** (`generators.json.gz`, from `new Rng(seed)` with seeds 0, 1, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 0x7fffffff, 0xffffffff): 32 `next()` as `u32` (the mulberry32 output before the division by 2^32) and as doubles, 8 `normal()` and the state after them, `poisson` for 12 means (0.3 to 120, with the draws each took: 1 to 26, and 2 above 30), 4 `range(2, 5)`, `FastRng` equal to `Rng` (true for every seed), the `FastRng.normal` sequence (with its cached sin variate) and `FastRng.poisson`. Seed 0 starts from the constant `0x9e3779b9`. Also the hashes and 28 sample entries of `NORMAL_TABLE` (4,096), `FAST_TABLES.esat/rateT/edk` and `EXPNEG_TABLE`, which `install.ts` reads from the modules' own exports.
- **In a run**: for the fire stream (seed + 1; draws through `Rng.next`) the first 256 draws with the consumer's label (`rollDebris` is the only consumer that draws in the 960 s runs); for the embers stream (seed; `Rng` holds only the state) the first 256 draws through `FastRng`, with labels, the totals, one event per `emit` and `step` call (`n` draws), and **per particle advance: the draws it consumed**, as `[slot, n]` pairs for the first 6,000 advances of each window and a histogram over the whole run. `n` is measured from the state of the generator (mulberry32 adds `0x6d2b79f5` per draw, so the number of draws is `(s1 - s0)` times the inverse of that constant modulo 2^32): it counts the inline copies in `advance` (EmberModel.ts:1602-1657), the `FastRng` calls inside it (`loft`, EmberModel.ts:1283, one draw per new particle; `land`, EmberModel.ts:1895-1907) and nothing else. `sumCheck` is true in all seven runs: the draws via `FastRng` plus the draws inline equal the generator's final state, so no consumer is missing. The atmosphere stream (seed + 3) draws **0** in every run, the explain stream is never created as a drawer, as the map says.
- `steps.json` also carries the cumulative draw counters of the three streams after every step, so a wrong draw count is located to a step.

Draw counts of the runs (seed 13): `katoomba3-fast` to 960 s: fire 200, embers 641,298 (107,435 particle advances); `katoomba3-standard`: fire 263, embers 1,476,429 (248,973); `-noembers`: fire 188 and 263, embers 0; `katoomba6-fast-4h`: fire 4,130, embers 7,149,356; `katoomba3-standard-4h`: fire 1,344, embers 7,311,874; `warrumbungles6-fast-4h`: fire 46, embers 3,801,806. Per particle step the draw count of the fast run in `w0` is 0 (3,324 of 6,071 particle steps), 1 (1,265), 3 (166), 4 (52), 6 (151), 9 (175), 12 (625), 16 (163) and a tail to 27: one wrong branch moves every later particle.

### 2.5 The steps tape (`steps.json`), not asked for, needed to locate a bug

After every `stepOnce`: `t`, `tEnd`, `dt`, the descriptors of every fire array (`arrivalTime`, `burnState`, `ros`, `intensity`, `flameHeight`, `spreadDir`, `driver`, `phase`), `moisture`, `heatPrev`, `crownPrev`, the draw counters, for embers `count`, `nAct` and one hash of all particle arrays, for the standard tier the hash of the air's `u, v, w, th, sm`. **Every one of the 96 (fast), 106 (standard) and 1,440 (`-4h`) steps is covered**, so a C# run is compared step by step and the first differing step and array is named, which is what the card's "bisecting at A15" lacked.

## 5. How the C# side reads it

Everything is JSON, codec frames and raw little-endian arrays; no new binary format.

1. **Codec frames** (`raw/*.bin`; the same layout as `init.bin`, so this is area 1's decoder): `u32` header length H, H bytes of UTF-8 JSON, zero padding so that the body starts on a multiple of 8, then the arrays back to back each starting on a multiple of 8. In the JSON a typed array is `{"$ta":"<kind>","o":<byte offset in the body>,"n":<elements>}` (kinds `f32 f64 i8 u8 u8c i16 u16 i32 u32 i64 u64`) and a number JSON cannot carry is `{"$num":"NaN"|"Infinity"|"-Infinity"|"-0"}`; an absent field is `undefined`. Root of a raw frame: `{probe, window, i, t, before:{args:[...], state:{...}|null}, after:{args:[...], ret, state}}`. In `args` an argument that is a function (a callback) or `undefined` is `null`; an instance of a class is a plain object with the extra key `$class`. `state` holds the named fields of `this` (`state` in `spec.mjs`; for `ember` and `fire` a digest layout, see `install.ts`).
2. **Descriptors** (every JSON file except the frames): a number (`-0`, NaN and infinities as `$num`), string, boolean, null, an array or object of descriptors, `{"$ta":kind,"n":elements,"h":"8 hex digits"[, "blob":{"o":byte offset in blobs.bin,"n":elements,"k":kind}]}` for a typed array, `{"$arr":"f64"|"mixed","n":N,"h":...}` for a plain array over 32 elements, `{"$undef":1}`, `{"$fn":1}`, `{"$map":size}`, `{"$class":"Name", ...own scalar and typed-array fields}`, `{"$skipped":1}`. **`h` is FNV-1a 32 over the array's bytes** (little endian, as stored): `h = 0x811c9dc5; for each byte: h ^= b; h = h * 16777619 mod 2^32`, written as 8 lower-case hex digits (the `fnv` of `src/sim/testing/hash.ts`, T4.1's hash); for `$arr f64` the bytes of the elements as float64. A C# test matches an actual value against a descriptor: a number by its bits (`double.Parse` of the JSON text is the exact value; compare `BitConverter.DoubleToInt64Bits`), an array by length and hash, an object key by key (ignore `$class`).
3. **`calls/A<n>.json`**: `{area, scenario, seed, probes:{"A5/headRosKernel":{"w0":[record...],"w900":[...]}}}`. A record: `i` (call index within the window), `t`, `a` (arguments before, in order), `z` (object `{argIndex: descriptor}` of the arguments the call changed), `r` (result), `s:{b,a}` (state of `this` before and after), `raw` (path of a raw frame). To prove a function: for a record with `raw`, decode the frame, call the function on `before.args` (and `before.state`), compare the changed arguments with `after.args` and `z`, the result with `after.ret`/`r`; for a record without `raw`, build the scalar arguments from `a` and compare with `z` and `r` (arrays by hash).
4. **`steps.json`**: `{steps:[{k,t,tEnd,dt,fire:{arrivalTime:descriptor,...},moisture,heatPrev,crownPrev,draws:{fire,embers,atmosphere},embers:{count,nAct,h},atm:{u,v,w,th,sm},emberLog:{wind:[from,to],turb:[..],landing:[..],ignite:[..]}}]}`; `draws` are cumulative draw counts of the three streams after the step. `k` counts `stepOnce` calls from 0; `t` is the start of the step.
5. **`env-tape.json`** (`{hostCalls, steps:[...]}`) and **`air-tape.json`** (`{steps:[...], ember:{...}, landing:{w0:[{x,y,r}],w900:[...]}, ignite:{...}}`): one entry per fire step, `k` as in `steps.json`; arrays as descriptors, with `blob` where kept. **`blobs.bin`**: read `n` elements of kind `k` at byte offset `o` (little endian). Compare the hash of what was read with `h` first (the self-test does).
6. **`ember-wind-<w>.f64`, `ember-turb-<w>.f64`**: float64 little endian, records of 6 (`x, y, zAGL, a, b, c`) in call order; `air-tape.json` `ember.<kind>.<w>` gives the record count, and `steps.json` `emberLog` the record range of each step. The C# ember model's `windFn` returns the next wind record **after checking that `x, y, zAGL` equal the record's bits**; a mismatch is a finding, not an error to skip.
7. **`draws.json`**: `streams.fire`/`streams.embers` (`first`, `totalDraws`, `byLabel`), `emberEvents` (`k` emit|step, `t`, `n`, `s0` the generator state before), `perParticleAdvance` (`pairs.<w>`: flat `[slot, draws, slot, draws, ...]`; `histogram`), `standalone.seeds.<seed>` and `tables`. The goldens in `goldens/` hold the same without `emberEvents` beyond 40 and without `pairs` beyond 200 entries.
8. **`index.json`**: per probe and window the call count (`calls`), recorded records, raw frames; `callSequence: {names:[...], init:[idx...], spin, w0, w600, w900}` the order of the per-step calls of `stepOnce` (indices into `names`).
`reader.mjs` is a working reference of items 1, 2, 5 and 6 (`decodeFrame`, `leafOfDecoded`, `fnvHex`, `openProbes().blob`, `emberLog`) and its `--selftest` runs items 1, 2 and 5 over every frame and blob of a folder.

## 7. Surprising, and what the plan or the map got wrong

1. **`PlumeField` is never reached.** `SIM_PARAMS.emberLoft` is `'briggs'` (`src/sim/params.ts:78`), so `EmberModel` lofts by Briggs in the standard tier too (`loftMode`, EmberModel.ts:429). `PlumeField.build` and `sample` have zero calls in all seven runs, including 4 h of the standard tier. Map section 1.1 item 6 and area A9 ("standard tier only, build it in M3, about 270 C# lines") are wrong for this commit: the plume loft is dead code at the gate settings. A decision for the orchestrator: port it only if the setting may change to `'auto'`.
2. **Never called in any of the seven runs** (two sites, fast and standard, embers on and off, to 14,400 s): `droughtState`, `droughtFactor`, `kbdiSeries` (the scenarios carry `kbdi` and the drought factor in `init.bin`, so the daily rainfall series is never read), `fhsFromLoad`, `barkHazardFromLoad` (`fuel/hazard.ts`), `pineCore` (no pine fuel at these sites), `breachProbability` and `breachDraw`, `loadFromIntensity`, `albiniFallHeight`, `pyroFirepowerThreshold`, `briggsRise` (`atmosphere/plume.ts`), `DiagnosticWind.wNight`. They are in the slice by the call graph; at the gate scenarios they cost nothing to leave out, but a different site or fuel will reach some of them (pine, breach).
3. **The ember draw count per particle step is not only inline.** The map (2.5) names the inline copies in `advance`; the draws inside `advance` also come from `loft` (one `FastRng.next` for a new particle: 16,635 of the 150,744 `FastRng` draws of the fast run are labelled `advance`) and `land` (3,520). The per-advance count is therefore the right unit to compare, and it is in the golden.
4. **`terrainDerived` is cached for the exporter's own terrain**, so a probe run of the unmodified exporter never recomputes the derived fields the way a reader of `init.bin` must (map 1.1 item 2). `install.ts` decodes the terrain from the same `init.bin` bytes once and calls `terrainDerived` on that copy so that `buildTerrain`, `sanitiseElevation` and `classifyLandforms` are recorded.
5. **Step lengths**: the fast tier steps at 10 s throughout (96 steps to 960 s); the standard tier mostly at 10 s but between 4.2 s and 12 s (106 steps to 960 s: 49 of 10 s, 11 of 12 s, 11 of 7.5 s, the rest the splits of an interval into equal steps), so 9.5 s of the map is an average.
6. **The environment tape the map imagined (26 MB for 60 steps) is smaller**: blobs are deduplicated by content (the moisture arrays change every 600 s and the wind every few steps), 6 MB for the 960 s of the fast run.
7. **Plan T3.4's "environment tape: the inputs the host feeds each step"** has no per-step host input to record in these scenarios (section 2.1). The tape is the environment `Simulation` hands the fire.

## 8. Not done, and limits

- Fixtures for seeds 12 and 14 and the 1,800 s horizon were not probed (the card asks for seed 13, two windows); `run-probes.mjs` has the seed in one constant.
- Raw frames exist only for the two main runs and only in the windows (`rawIn` in `spec.mjs` adds `w600` for moisture); a call outside a window is counted but its arguments are not kept. The `-4h` runs keep tapes, counters and hashes, no frames.
- No C# was written or run: the claim that a frame is enough to replay a call is checked only in that every frame decodes and agrees with its descriptors (`--selftest`), not by replaying a TypeScript function from it.
- The probes cost 2 to 9 times the run time; they are for generating the files, never for timing.
- Disk: the scratch of this card peaked near 700 MB; it was removed when the card was done (`df` at the start showed 7.5 GB free, below the 8 GB the brief asks to keep, so nothing large was kept).
